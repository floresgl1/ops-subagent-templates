The problem

Your scheduled job ran last night. Did it actually work?

Scheduled systems fail silently. You scan logs, check output files, cross-reference timestamps—every morning, manually. You find out something broke when a stakeholder asks why the data is stale.

Without triage

Stale data for hours before anyone notices. Root-causing takes the whole morning because nobody checked the pipeline output until it was needed.

With this template

A health script gathers the facts. A Claude sub-agent reads them, reasons about what went wrong, and delivers a verdict before you open your laptop.

Architecture

Two layers, one daily check

A plain Python script gathers the facts. A Claude sub-agent reads the output and reasons about what happened. Neither layer works without the other.

health-snapshot.py

Gathers facts from your system: output file existence, task exit codes, cross-check invariants. Runs standalone—no AI dependencies.

↓
daily-triage agent

Reads the health output, digs into logs when something looks off, and produces a structured verdict: HEALTHY, DEGRADED, or FAILED.

Read-only by design. The sub-agent's tools are scoped to Bash, Read, Grep, and Glob. It cannot modify files, call APIs, or change your system. Belt and suspenders: tool scoping plus a hard rule in the agent prompt.
